## Comparison with the Ordnance Survey's farthest point from the sea

The Ordnance Survey placed Great Britain's farthest point from the sea at Church Flatts Farm near Coton in the Elms, Derbyshire, about 70 miles (113 km) from the sea at mean low water (BBC News, 23 July 2003). The farthest square from this study's coast is 114.4 km from it, near Akeley (Buckinghamshire), 90 km from the Ordnance Survey point, because OpenStreetMap's coastline runs up tidal estuaries and rivers. The paper reports this as a planned check that the coastline definition fails.

## Summary of the contribution (for a cover letter)

Sea views are valued in housing markets and associated with well-being in blue-space research, but I have found no national estimate of where the sea is visible for the United Kingdom. This paper computes one: 360 sight lines from every 1 km square (244,595 squares) over open terrain data to 50 km, with curvature and refraction, against OpenStreetMap's sea (including tidal estuaries and sea lochs). Standing on bare ground, 26.3% of UK land has a sea view (41% of Scotland, 16% of England), rising to 29.2% at 5 m; 22.6% of Great Britain's population lives at settlement points with a view. Half of low-lying squares lose the sea within 3.5 km of the coast. Every figure is an upper bound for bare terrain, and the headline is resolution-sensitive (finer terrain lowers it by up to 7.3 points in test windows). Every check was pre-specified in a written plan; documented summit views, alternative distances, refraction, sea definitions, resolution and simulated terrain error test the result. A per-square indicator and open code are released for use in coastal amenity and health research.
